# Use AI to challenge judgment without outsourcing learning

A model can help a person generate alternatives or interrogate a decision. That is different from learning the underlying skill or establishing that an answer is true. A good learning exercise preserves room for the learner to think, make mistakes and receive feedback.

## Form a view, then invite challenge

The host recommends forming a tentative judgment before consulting AI.

Young Zhao describes recording decisions and asking a model for feedback; Mo Gawdat describes comparing models to expose competing assumptions.

Shishir Mehrotra emphasizes learning to evaluate output and practicing creative judgment with quick feedback, rather than treating tool operation as the whole skill.

**Editorial application:** Write down your first answer and why you believe it, ask for an alternative interpretation, and independently check consequential factual claims. Different models can share errors; a confident challenge is still only a candidate for examination.

## Separate performance from learning

Andrew Ng warns that AI-assisted homework may improve immediate performance without producing retention, although the interview does not establish the underlying studies or limits of that claim.

Fei-Fei Li emphasizes education's social and human-development purposes alongside tool use.

Brian Chesky describes developing ideas himself and using AI to surface patterns before editing.

The tradeoff is immediate assistance versus independent practice: delegating an answer may save effort now while leaving less opportunity to test what one remembers later. That is a plausible instructional concern, not proof of cognitive decline from everyday use.

## Learn with another person

David Rogier describes walking hesitant colleagues through a research task, while Li suggests asking someone trusted to demonstrate their use of AI.

Such examples are not all the same: one is guided task practice and another is a request for a demonstration. An exploratory conversation about a person's work can also precede choosing a task. **Open questions:** Can the learner reproduce the reasoning later without assistance? Whose feedback is available?
