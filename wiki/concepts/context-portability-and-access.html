Portable context, permissions and provider dependence

An assistant's value may depend on accumulated information about a team, its customers and its work. Moving that context between tools and controlling who can see it are related problems, but solving one does not solve the other.

Preserve useful context

The host proposes a shared knowledge layer containing audience, voice, performance and strategy, with separate instructions for individual tasks.

The Lieberman brothers argue that accumulated assistant memory and integrations with email and calendars can make changing providers difficult.

Editorial synthesis: keeping an authorized, understandable copy of reusable instructions outside a single assistant may make some context easier to transfer. A folder alone does not guarantee that another tool can use the same integrations, preserve behavior or switch at low cost. Nor does it settle how the workflow should be revised; that belongs to workflow learning.

Portability is not permission

A discussion of Kalshi's planning system raises questions about access to sensitive legal and surveillance information.

Andrew Ng recommends examining provider terms and data retention, and sometimes choosing a local model or avoiding AI for sensitive material.

As an editorial precaution, identify the people entitled to access each category, restrict credentials and review whether the purpose justifies sharing it. A portable shared store can reduce dependence on one provider while creating another concentrated repository of sensitive data. Local processing is a choice with its own operational risks, not automatic confidentiality. External actions require separate approval boundaries.

Alternatives are hypotheses

The Lieberman brothers advocate independent and decentralized alternatives and are themselves building Gonka.

Their proposal does not establish that distributed infrastructure can economically and reliably support frontier AI. The tradeoff between integrated convenience and the ability to leave a provider remains context-dependent.

Open questions: Which context is essential to move, and in what format? Can permissions survive export and deletion? Who checks provider retention terms and audits changes? What happens to the workflow if a connector is withdrawn?
