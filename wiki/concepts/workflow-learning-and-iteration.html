Turn manual work into reviewed, reusable workflows

One practical starting point for a reusable AI workflow is a task somebody already understands, rather than a promise that an entire company will run itself. As an editorial heuristic, define a sequence with known inputs, an output someone can inspect, and a way to revise instructions when that output misses the mark.

Start with a real task

Greg Brockman recommends trying a small, recurring problem manually several times before automating it; his suggested repetition count is a rule of thumb, not a validated threshold.

The host separates defining, executing and evaluating work when considering which part might be assisted.

Editorial application: Choose one bounded step, record what a satisfactory result needs, and compare the assisted attempt with the existing process. Repetition gives a team examples to inspect; it does not by itself prove that automation saves time or improves quality.

Capture context and revise

Peter Yang describes recording a workflow and reviewing AI-proposed changes to its instructions.

In a Codex demonstration, the host asked for per-post detail after seeing the first spreadsheet, prompting another iteration.

The host describes separating shared audience and business context from task-specific instructions; a recurring guest-research workflow prepares an outreach draft for a producer to adapt.

Her proposed loop that converts editorial decisions and performance data into actionable briefs was still unfinished.

Measure outcomes, not activity

Max Jaderberg says a research benchmark should be revised if better scores fail to track progress on live discovery; Andrew Ng recommends measures suited to the business, such as retention, speed or accuracy.

A model score or count of generated items can rise without the downstream outcome improving; that is a hypothetical, not an observed result established across these examples. Editorial recommendation: pair a workflow measure with a meaningful outcome and a baseline, and decide who will review exceptions. For consequential actions see approval boundaries; for where reusable knowledge lives see context and permissions.

Open questions: What would make the team discard the workflow? Can the comparison account for correction time and changing task difficulty? Does a performance alert lead to a decision rather than another dashboard?
