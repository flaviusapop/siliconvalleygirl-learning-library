From cheap prototype to durable customer value

Building a convincing prototype answers one question: can this interaction be shown? A business also has to discover a meaningful problem, reach people who have it, deliver reliably and earn enough to continue. None of those follows automatically from a fast demo.

Test a specific pain

Anton Osika recommends talking to potential customers and looking for initial purchase and repeat use.

The host discusses narrow business ideas and a specialized interface built on general AI capabilities; an unanswered call can suggest a problem to investigate, but does not establish willingness to pay.

Editorial application: Before expanding the build, ask a prospective user how they solve the problem now, what failure costs them and whether they would actually try or buy a solution. A single positive conversation is a lead, not validation.

Reach and keep users

Greg Brockman argues that durable healthcare products require domain understanding and trust among patients, clinicians, administrators and insurers.

Gary Vaynerchuk imagines a small subscription app distributed through organic content and argues brand and discovery still matter; the host counters that a large AI company could copy its features.

These views point to an unresolved tradeoff: broad models may lower development costs while also increasing competitive pressure. Distribution, continued use and customer trust are hypotheses to test, not guaranteed defenses.

Prototype is not production

Fei-Fei Li sees product managers using AI prototypes to shorten feedback cycles while designers and engineers address more sophisticated work.

An OpenAI product leader distinguishes a small prototype from scaling and maintaining a service, where technical expertise remains useful.

The host's Duolingo example involved an existing puzzle database, some technical experience and engineers helping with the final product.

Open questions: Do customers return and pay after novelty fades? What are support, infrastructure and acquisition costs? Can the team maintain the product when inputs or models change? See task-centered interfaces for interaction design and workflow iteration for measurement.
