His examples include natural-language search that sets filters, personalized listing highlights, questions answered from a listing, side-by-side comparisons and neighborhood information. He argues that travel shopping is visual and collaborative: people need to browse, compare, share and contact hosts as well as express a request conversationally. He calls an agent an intelligent application that can understand a user, whereas a chatbot is an interface; interoperability and a host-side pricing coach are part of his forward-looking vision. ## Actionable learnings

1. For a comparison-heavy product, test a visual interface with filters and side-by-side choices instead of assuming chat alone is sufficient.
2. Pilot a service in a limited market and evaluate customer usefulness and operational quality before expanding.
3. Record ideas without immediate critique, select promising entries regularly and use AI to surface patterns before editing the strongest ones.
4. Schedule a bounded period for industry news and protect a separate uninterrupted practice block; ask an experienced user for help trying a new tool.
