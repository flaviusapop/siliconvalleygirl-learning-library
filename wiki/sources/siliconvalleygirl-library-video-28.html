Summary

The conversation opens with a practical idea for improving a reusable AI workflow: after using an instruction file, ask the assistant to suggest updates based on the conversation and review the changes before using them again. The guest says he spent focused time describing his manual creator workflows to an AI coding tool. His examples include preparing podcasts, editing newsletter drafts, adapting material for social platforms and producing recurring reports on his content and other channels. For posts, he describes dictating his own ideas, using examples of previous work to help polish them, checking the result and then authorizing distribution. He says he still works with a human video editor and uses AI to suggest possible cuts and introductory excerpts.

He describes five levels of AI use: asking everyday questions; using AI for routine work; prototyping products; building apps, often for personal use; and connecting an agent to automate workflows. He prefers tools that can work with files and integrations rather than requiring repeated copying between a chat and other applications. In his account, individual instruction files can be chained for research, writing, podcast preparation and administrative work. He also warns that an early working version is not the same as a polished, tested product. His claims about time savings and capability are personal experience and opinion, not measured results established by the transcript.

The personal-advisor example combines a short document about goals, principles, business context and sources of energy with an instruction file. A separate learnings file stores concise observations from past conversations, and the assistant is directed to ask whether a proposed observation should be saved. He says the skill does not always trigger automatically and that he sometimes invokes it directly. He also describes checking instruction files for duplication and keeping them short enough to read. The host explores a possible chief-of-staff workflow that could draw on team messages and meeting records; this is a proposed use, not evidence of a deployed or validated system. Connecting personal or team information warrants a separate review of access, privacy and permissions.

Their suggested starting point is to choose a tool, describe recurring tasks and gradually build and refine workflows, allowing for setup and repeated feedback. The guest argues that direct experimentation can be more useful than consuming AI news, but acknowledges that the tools do not reliably deliver exactly what he wants in one attempt. The closing conversation is more ambivalent: he worries about feeling less able to work without an AI partner, and both discuss how children might retain foundational skills. He also worries that mass-produced content can spread despite low quality, and places value on personal principles and human judgment.
