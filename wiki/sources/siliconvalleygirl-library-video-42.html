Summary

The host asks the Duolingo CEO how the company applies AI. He describes engineers changing coding workflows and product managers building prototypes to communicate ideas, rather than relying only on written proposals. A company-wide coding exercise and employee channels for practices and failures are presented as ways to learn from colleagues. He says a prior plan to make AI use part of performance reviews was reversed: outcomes should matter more than using a tool when it does not fit. His account is a company leader’s description, not an independent assessment of implementation or employee experience.

As an example, he describes two employees developing an initial chess-course prototype and curriculum with AI. His fuller account qualifies the opening setup: one employee had some technical knowledge, and engineers helped produce the version included in the app. The employees researched existing learning tools, built chess puzzles, and iterated on mobile prototypes. He says AI struggled with puzzle generation and describes using an existing puzzle database to improve it. He encourages beginners to experiment while learning basic programming concepts. He also says AI-generated code can be hard to debug when it fails and generated stories may require checking or spot-checking. He does not claim a uniform company-wide productivity multiplier; gains are uneven and collaboration and existing code remain constraints.

The discussion then turns to AI translation and competition. The CEO believes many people learn languages for enjoyment or practical reasons that translation alone will not remove, while acknowledging that some uses and translation work may change. He expects user expectations for intelligent features to rise and describes plans to make conversation practice available at lower price tiers as costs fall; these are company plans, not guarantees. He says he uses AI for research but retains responsibility for decisions. The host separately promotes a meeting-notes product and her newsletter; the product claims are promotional and unverified.

The CEO says the company has not conducted layoffs and frames continued hiring as valuable when workers are productive; this is his statement, not an independently checked employment history. He describes shifting toward user growth even at a near-term monetization cost, acknowledging investor resistance. On future jobs he expresses uncertainty: he expects some roles to change and some employers to need fewer staff, while seeing continuing human roles in teaching and project management. His comments about future workforce demand and company performance are forecasts, not investment or career guarantees. He also describes using a longer time horizon to put setbacks in perspective and notes that he still reacts emotionally to company metrics.
