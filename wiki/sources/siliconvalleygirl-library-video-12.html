Summary

Osika argues that AI-assisted building is moving beyond quick demonstrations toward operating businesses, a position tied directly to his role as Lovable's co-founder. His method starts with a real task done manually: split it into pieces, test each for value and repair failure points instead of overengineering a complete system. He says people with professional experience can notice neglected problems because they encounter them daily, and suggests combining software with a real-world service. His example of AI dog portraits includes payment, marketing and delivery of a physical portrait; the host's $300,000 monthly figure is an unverified anecdotal business claim, not evidence that similar products earn that amount. To gauge demand he proposes asking ten prospective customers about their pain, getting one to pay, and building a base of paying fans who may refer others. At the end he adds an important condition: customers must return and keep paying, not merely buy once from excitement, before the founder treats the signal as product-market fit.

The working session begins with a redesign of the host's site using its existing URL and her description of the brand. In parallel, Osika restates her problem as a lead-capture form where anyone can describe an opportunity, an inbox ranks submissions by relevance, and AI proposes responses the team can edit. Actionable learnings

  1. Map an existing manual workflow and test one automated component against the actual task before adding more.
  2. Interview ten prospective customers about their problem and test whether at least one will pay; then track repeat use and payment.
  3. Prototype an intake form and ranked inbox with example data, keeping outbound replies behind explicit human approval.
  4. Diagnose whether poor traction comes from discovery, customer fit, defects or retention before deciding to persist or pivot.
